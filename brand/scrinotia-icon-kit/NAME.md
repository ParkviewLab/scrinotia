# The name: Scrinotia

**Scrinotia**, from Latin *scrinium* (scroll-case) and *notitia* (knowledge, record), with haplology: "the scroll-case of knowledge".

**Pronunciation:** skri · NO · tee · a (/skrɪˈnoʊ.ti.ə/)

## The two roots

### *scrinium*: the scroll-case

A *scrinium* was a cylindrical case or chest in which Romans kept scrolls, letters and documents. Under the later empire the word also named the imperial secretariats, the offices that kept the state's records. Their keeper was the *scriniarius*.

English **"shrine"** descends from *scrinium*, through Old English *scrīn*, originally a chest for precious things or relics. A vault of markdown files is exactly that: a case that holds writing worth keeping.

*Scrinium* is probably **not** related to *scribere* ("to write"); its origin is uncertain. Many readers will still hear "scribe" in the name, which suits a writing tool.

### *notitia*: knowledge, record

*Notitia* comes from *noscere* ("to come to know"), the same root as *nota* (note, mark) and *notio* (notion, idea). It has two senses, and the app uses both:

1. **knowledge, awareness, acquaintance**: what is known.
2. **a record, register or catalogue**: a written account of what is known. The best-known example is the *Notitia Dignitatum*, the late-Roman register of offices.

Knowledge plus register is a close description of a markdown vault, and of the agent-readable knowledge bases built on top of one.

## How *scrinium* + *notitia* becomes *Scrinotia*

The full join would be *scri-no-ti-tia*. Two neighbouring, near-identical syllables (*-ti-tia*) collapse into one, giving *scri-no-tia*. Linguists call this **haplology**: dropping one of two similar adjacent syllables. Latin did it itself; *nutrix* ("nurse") comes from an earlier *nutri-trix*. English does it in speech, as when "probably" becomes "probly".

The ending **-ia** is the standard Latin suffix for abstract and collective nouns (*memoria*, *scientia*, *notitia*) and for places (*Italia*, *Gallia*). So the name also reads as a realm: the place where the knowledge is kept.

*Scrinotia* is a modern coinage, not a classical word. It's built from real Latin parts by a documented process, in the same spirit as the sibling projects **PensaGrex** and **PensaForma**.

## Why "tee-a" and not "shee-a"

In classical Latin *t* is always hard, so *notitia* was "no-TI-ti-a". The "sh" sound (as in *nation*) arose much later, through medieval Latin and English. Saying **tee** keeps the name consistent with classical Latin, matches its spelling, and keeps it from drifting toward "notion" (and the app *Notion*).

## How the name was chosen

The working title was *granite-vaulting*. The rest of the shortlist and why each was set aside:

| Candidate | Why not |
|---|---|
| NotaScrinium | Too close to the existing note app at scrinium-note.com |
| Notatheca | A Latin–Greek hybrid (*theca* is Greek) |
| Arcanota | Sounds almost the same as Arcanotes (arcanotes.com), another notes product |
| Scrinota | Used by a 2022 CS50 Chrome extension for timers and note-taking |
| Capsota | An everyday Catalan word for "big, clumsy box" |
| Scrinotio | A near-rhyme with *Pinocchio*, a poor association for an AI knowledge tool |
| Notioscrin / Notiascrin | Opens like *Notion*; the "-scrin" ending reads as "screen" |

Scrinotia came out clean: no exact matches on the open web at the time of naming (October 2026). Re-check GitHub, the app stores and the USPTO trademark database (TESS) before any public launch or filing.

## The icon

The icon is a mid-century Googie "sputnik": a sky-blue ball carrying a markdown `#`, ten apricot-tipped spokes, and an ultramarine orbit of speed dashes ending in a small satellite, on a midnight-blue ground with gold sparkles. The ball is the case; the starburst is the knowledge radiating from it; the orbit is the agents and tools that circle the vault.

## Short forms

Use the full name in prose. For identifiers, `scrinotia` (repo, bundle-ID suffix, command-line tool) is preferred. Avoid shortening it to "Nota" or "Scrin". "Nota" is crowded with existing notes apps, and "Scrin" reads as "screen".
